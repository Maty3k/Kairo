# M1 · Test instruments

*Opened 2026-10-23 · Size M · Plan: [PLAN §4, M1](../PLAN.md#m1--test-instruments-m) · M1 closes when every gate check passes and you say "pass".*

## Goal

Machines can check recordings before any spike or engine code is written. Claude builds the instruments that M2's spikes and M3's engine are measured with. The testbed, `kairo-verify`, loadgen and bench are outside the exit-rule clock; days on `wgc-probe` and `audio-soak` count toward it.

## Deliverables (from the plan)

- `testbed.exe` (crate `kairo-testbed`): windowed, borderless or exclusive fullscreen, 60–360 fps; a block barcode that survives scaling and H.264, moving bars, a per-second flash with a 1 kHz "game" beep, HDR patches and a QPC log as ground truth. A separate process plays a 2 kHz "voice" beep.
- `kairo-verify` (ffprobe plus its own analysis): drops and duplicates (≤0.5% [proposed]), content cadence, CFR, A/V offset per track ≤40 ms, IDR at 0, closed 1 s GOP, pts = dts, moov first, no edit list, `colr`, decoded bars ≥40 dB PSNR (catches silent encoder corruption), routing (voice tone only on T4; Kairo's cue absent), tone SNR, curve levels, export profiles; nothing GPL linked.
- `kairo-loadgen`: a deterministic GPU load with ALU and bandwidth modes ([research 13](../research/13-amd-performance.md) §5.5), borderless on the OLED (see Your part, step 6), scriptable.
- `wgc-probe`: Kairo's own Windows.Graphics.Capture module per [research 08](../research/08-capture-rust.md), later the engine's, with a probe for frame-delivery traces and a capability report.
- `audio-soak`: [research 10](../research/10-audio-rust.md)'s tone-marker soak (§8.3) and noise detector. M1's dry runs leave out its optional microphone source, so nothing in M1 opens your mic.
- `hotkey-probe`, for SP5: watches Kairo's default keys and mouse side buttons and logs each press with its QPC time and the foreground window's elevation. AMD's hotkeys come from the registry, since a successful `RegisterHotKey` proves nothing against AMD's in-game hook.
- `pc bench` with `pm-stats.ps1`, `ab-stats.ps1` and `encoder-usage.ps1` ([PLAN §3.2](../PLAN.md#32-testing-strategy); research 13 §5 and §9).
- `pc harness`: testbed, capture and `kairo-verify`, unattended. In M1 an FFmpeg CLI capture stands in for the engine.
- The AMD settings diff script (research 13 §4.2).
- On the Mac: the `kairo-core` and `kairo-mock-engine` skeletons.

|---|---|---|

How the bad clips are made, and whether one dropped frame fails a clip, are open (see Unverified items) |
| 2 | Unattended testbed + FFmpeg CLI capture → a verify report in <5 min | With no one at the keyboard (see [How M1 jobs run on the PC](#how-m1-jobs-run-on-the-pc)), `pc harness` starts one `\Kairo\Run` job (only one runs at a time) that opens the testbed on the 180 Hz monitor and records it with the FFmpeg CLI (`gfxcapture` or `ddagrab` into `h264_amf`: NV12, every option set and read back, peak ≤50 Mbps), then runs `kairo-verify`; Claude times it from start to report. | 3 | `wgc-probe` traces from both monitors | On each monitor, one `\Kairo\Run` job starts the testbed as moving content and `wgc-probe` together; `wgc-probe` writes a trace of every delivered frame's `SystemRelativeTime` (see Formats; the deltas are the differences between consecutive `ts`) plus the capability report: adapters, monitors with refresh rate and HDR state, the default `MinUpdateInterval`, the borderless-access status. **Formats.**
- `audio-soak` (research 10 §8.3): `markers.jsonl` holds each marker's QPC render time; `metrics.jsonl` holds noise, timing and health for each source every second, plus the capturing process's CPU use, private bytes, handle count and thread count once a minute. Research 10's launch sketch also follows an `events.jsonl` (§8.1) without saying what it holds, and every soak logs System events 41, 1074 and 6008 (PLAN §3.1); Claude decides which file gets them and names it here.
- `pm-stats.ps1 -Csv -Exe -Tag` prints one JSON line per CSV: `tag`, `frames`, `avg_fps`, `low1_fps` (1000 ÷ P99, nearest rank), `low01_fps`, `gpu_busy_ms`, `gpu_wait_ms`, `present_modes`, `frame_types`. `ab-stats.ps1 -A -B` prints `n pairs: loss m% (95% CI lo..hi%) PASS=…`; research 13's version tests only the mean (≤3%) and the upper bound (≤4%), so the plan's other clauses join before SP1.
- `encoder-usage.ps1 -Seconds` lists video-engine use per process, adapter LUID and engine type, with file names only: research 13's sketch prints full paths and merges the two adapters.
- `env.json` (research 13 §9, plus whether AMD Software is installed) holds PC details, so it is never published, even though research 13 §9 says to publish it with the bench summary (CLAUDE.md: never commit PC details).
- The AMD diff script runs as two calls, before and after you toggle: scripts reach the PC on stdin, so a `Read-Host` pause can't get your key press. It prints `path\name: before -> after` lines and only reads.
- `wgc-probe` traces (`kairo-core`'s `trace` module) stay replayable offline, because `kairo-core`'s pacer is tested on real SP2 traces (PLAN §3.2). A trace is UTF-8 text: the line `# kairo-trace 1`, then one line per frame WGC delivered, in arrival order, holding `ts arr ready` in 100 ns counted from the first frame's `SystemRelativeTime`. `ts` is the frame's `SystemRelativeTime`; `arr` is when its `FrameArrived` handler ran; `ready` is when it reached the pacer, or `-` if it never did, because a newer frame replaced it in the probe's mailbox or the probe dropped it while taking it, so the probe's own drops can't pass for gaps in what WGC delivered. Other lines starting with `#` are comments. A trace, comments included, holds no monitor, adapter or PC names, refresh rate, interval or wall-clock time; those stay with the job's record in `.dogfood/`. The probe writes each line in one piece, so a run stopped midway leaves whole lines.
- `kairo-verify` reports: Claude decides the layout at implementation time.

**Mac.** `tools/mac/pc` gains the verbs `bench` and `harness`; like the M0 verbs, they send their PowerShell to the PC on stdin. The mock engine serves `kairo-engine.sock` in the Mac's per-user temp folder with `kairo-hello`'s framing (JSON-RPC 2.0, a 4-byte big-endian length prefix, at most 1 MiB), `engine.hello` first; `kairo-rpc` v0.1 itself is M3 task 1. Code may be copied from `docs/research/prototypes/`, never depended on.

## How M1 jobs run on the PC

- **First.** `pc status`, and again before each long step: BUSY (today: your keyboard or mouse input in the last 10 minutes, or a fullscreen window in front; a windowed game alone doesn't count until the games list lands, and nothing saves yet) or UNKNOWN means Claude works on the Mac. `pc doctor` each session. Capture needs you signed in and unlocked, the displays on and no Remote Desktop. Deploys happen only between your sessions.
- **Launch.** GUI, capture and audio programs start only through `\Kairo\Run`: in your session, at priority 4, with no console window. `kairo-devrun` refuses jobs in the SSH session. PresentMon may run from SSH, because its tracing is system-wide (cookbook §10.4).
- **Limits.** Every job has `max_seconds` and keeps `abort_on_input` on unless you're meant to use its window. One job at a time; exit 6 is the safety stop working. Nothing overnight unless you say so. Jobs write only in `C:\kairo\logs` (and `state`).
- **Screens and sound.** Patterns, harness runs and soaks use the 180 Hz monitor. The OLED shows only moving content, at most 10 minutes per run, under a black, capture-excluded cover when you're away. Tones play at −30 dBFS, the soak tone too (research 10 used −20).
- **Encoding.** AMF gets NV12 only, every option set and read back, H.264 peak ≤50 Mbps. One H.264 session at a time, each released at least 1 s before the next: back-to-back sessions have hung an RX 9000-series card (Sunshine #5385). Capture goes only through WGC (`gfxcapture`) or DDA (`ddagrab`), never `vsrc_amf` (house rule 2).
- **No synthetic input.** HDR toggles are scripts; SP5's key presses come from you. No M1 job needs a game. Any anti-cheat warning: stop, remove, report.
- **Privacy.** Never log clips, audio, typed keys, non-game window titles or raw paths; committed files get summaries. CI's house-rules scan (banned Windows calls and network code) reads only the Rust files and `Cargo.toml` files under `crates/`, and no CI scan looks for these logging rules in any language. So the PowerShell scripts keep the Never list unaided, and every program, PowerShell or Rust, keeps these privacy rules without a CI scan to catch a slip.
- **Not built yet.** Today `kairo-devrun` stops a job only at its time limit or your input. Before any job runs while you're away, Claude adds a stop within 2 s of a game start or another fullscreen window (not the job's own testbed or loadgen window, which `watch.json` reports as busy today), restores displays and audio, keeps the PC awake with the displays on, and covers the OLED. Until then, no job runs while you're away: Claude runs only short jobs while you're home and have said in chat that the PC is free, with `abort_on_input` on; on the OLED, only moving content, at most 10 minutes.

## The exit-rule clock

The rule is in CLAUDE.md, [PLAN §4, M3](../PLAN.md#4-milestones) (decision 26) and [ADR-0001](../adr/0001-architecture-and-stack.md); its counting is a plan proposal you can veto at a gate.

- **Start.** The first `wgc-probe` commit. Not yet: there is no `wgc-probe` code and no `docs/spikes/`.
- **What counts in M1.** Days on `wgc-probe` and `audio-soak`; not the testbed, `kairo-verify`, loadgen or bench. Cookbook §11 says otherwise: it puts its prerequisites, `wgc-probe`'s traces among them, "outside the exit-rule clock"; the plan's M1 goal, decision 26 and CLAUDE.md count `wgc-probe` days, and Claude follows them. The plan leaves `hotkey-probe`, `pc harness`, the AMD diff script and the Mac skeletons open; the rule covers "Rust capture, encode or audio code", so Claude counts all of the `kairo-core` skeleton's maths (the plan puts the pacer, ring and cut planning in the capture pipeline and the aligner, mixer and limiter in the audio pipeline, PLAN §2.2–2.3) and treats `hotkey-probe`, `pc harness`, the AMD diff script and the mock engine as outside.
- **A counted day.** At least 4 hours of engine work, with the PC available for at least 2 hours. A day is excluded only if the PC was unavailable or the blocker reproduces with the FFmpeg CLI or a C++ sample, logged that day with evidence.
- **Signals and steps.** Loop signals: the same compile, borrow or COM error class unresolved after 3 attempts, or an `unsafe` crash unresolved after 1 day. From counted day 3, a one-line daily summary to you. Day 4 without an end-to-end hard path: Claude drafts the C# plan and asks you before installing the .NET 10 SDK on the Mac and the PC. Day 5: a burn-down note listing every exclusion.
- **The switch.** At 7 counted days, or 14 calendar days after M3 starts, without M3's timed gate, Claude switches the engine to C#/.NET 10 NativeAOT unless you reply "keep Rust". The UI, contract, fixtures, mock and harness stay. A missed target that isn't Rust-specific gets its own ADR instead.
- **Budget.** M3's tasks are budgeted at about 6.25 of the 7 counted days (cookbook §11), so less than one day is spare: the first counted M1 or M2 day uses it all, and each one after that comes out of M3's own budget. Claude does the Mac-only pieces (ring, aligner, mixer, cut maths) before the first `wgc-probe` commit or alongside.
- **The day log**, `docs/spikes/engine-log.md`, is public, kept by Claude and starts with the first `wgc-probe` commit. Per engine day: the date; counted, excluded or neither; hours of engine work, and whether the PC was available for at least 2 hours (yes or no; the hours come from your activity, so they stay in `.dogfood/`); which code; the running total; loop-signal tallies; for an exclusion, the reason and a short evidence summary, with raw output in `.dogfood/`. It also records that first commit's date (squash merges hide it) and M3's start. Day 1 sets out Claude's reading of the edge cases for your veto: the code that counts, PC hours from Claude's own `pc status` checks, the day 3–5 steps applying before M3 (read literally, they do), and M2 days counting only with 4 hours of Rust capture, encode or audio work.

## Risks → fallbacks

- Barcode lost in compression → bigger blocks.
- Early Rust/COM friction → reported before M3; those days count.
- Dev work disturbs your gaming or wears the OLED → the watch agent, abort on input, the black OLED cover, patterns on the 180 Hz monitor.
- The runner can't yet stop on a game start, restore displays and audio, keep the PC awake or cover the OLED → no jobs while you're away until it can; until then, only short ones after your OK in chat.
- An AMF session hangs or never recovers (both seen on RX 9000-series cards) → one H.264 session at a time, at least 1 s apart; `max_seconds` ends the job; a driver timeout is P0: stop and report.
- AMF won't open on your PC → `h264_mf` (the plan's M3 fallback); it takes the first hardware encoder with no adapter filter, so Claude checks which one.
- AMD Software stays uninstalled → M1 still closes, since its A/A data needs none; the diff script's check, the Instant Replay comparison and SP5's "AMD on" wait.
- A Windows, driver or Adrenalin update lands mid-milestone → a harness smoke at the next idle window; `env.json` keeps data from before and after apart.
